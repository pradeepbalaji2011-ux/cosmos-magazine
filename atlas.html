Data sources & licences

Planet orbits: JPL “Keplerian elements for approximate positions of the major planets” (E. M. Standish), valid 1800–2050. NASA/JPL, public domain.

Stars: a hand-curated table of ~110 bright and famous stars (rounded values from standard catalogues such as Hipparcos and the Yale Bright Star Catalogue). Optional full catalogue: the HYG database by David Nash (astronexus.com), CC BY-SA, built from Hipparcos, Yale Bright Star and Gliese. Place it at data/hyg.csv.

Constellations: the 88 IAU names. Embedded figures (about 20) are simplified stick figures. Optional full set: data/constellations.lines.json from d3-celestial (BSD-3-Clause, check its licence before use).

Planet surfaces: optional real maps placed in textures/ (see textures/README.md). Recommended: Solar System Scope 2k textures (solarsystemscope.com/textures), CC BY 4.0, based on NASA imagery and elevation data; or public-domain NASA / USGS maps. Without them the atlas draws procedural textures, which are illustrative. Earth’s night-side lights are random and illustrative unless earth_night is supplied. Atmospheres, Saturn’s ring shadow and all shaders are stylised models written for this atlas.

Nebulae and clusters (Orion, Crab, Eagle, Ring, Helix, Horsehead, Carina; Pleiades, Hyades, Omega Centauri): positions, distances, sizes and facts are from standard references (Messier/NGC catalogues, Gaia-era distances, rounded). The gas and star particles are a stylised illustration, not images or real member positions.

Exoplanets (TRAPPIST-1, Proxima, Kepler-452, Kepler-186, 51 Pegasi, HD 209458): periods, radii and masses rounded from the NASA Exoplanet Archive and discovery papers (e.g. Agol et al. 2021 for TRAPPIST-1); orbit sizes are derived from Kepler’s third law. Habitable zone: conservative limits of Kopparapu et al. 2013. Orbital phase, orientation and planet looks are illustrative.

Black holes (Sagittarius A*, M87*, Cygnus X-1): mass and distance from the Event Horizon Telescope collaboration (2019, 2022), GRAVITY/Keck stellar orbits and Miller-Jones et al. 2021. Disk and lensing are an artistic ray-traced model of a non-spinning hole. Milky Way: the Sun’s distance to the centre (8.18 kpc) from GRAVITY 2019, bar angle (about 27°) from Bland-Hawthorn & Gerhard 2016, orientation from the IAU galactic coordinates. The four logarithmic spiral arms (pitch about 13°) and the Orion Spur are a schematic layout inspired by Reid et al. 2019, not a map of individual stars.

Galaxies and clusters: about 53 named galaxies and 10 clusters at real positions (RA, Dec, distance and size rounded from NED, SIMBAD and Wikipedia-level references; distances carry roughly 10% uncertainty). Galaxy survey: optional data/2mrs.csv from the 2MASS Redshift Survey (Huchra et al. 2012, VizieR J/ApJS/199/26, free for research with citation). Distances use cz/H0 with H0 = 70 km/s/Mpc, which is unreliable for the nearest galaxies. Galaxy icons, the look-back shell and the cosmic microwave background shell (about 45 billion light-years, comoving) are illustrative graphics.

Code: three.js r128 (MIT).
